# Data Privacy

Research data often contains personal information, from names and contact details to sensitive information about health, ethnicity or other protected characteristics.
Handling this data requires awareness of data protection law, ethical review and techniques such as anonymisation and pseudonymisation.

This page lists courses that teach how to manage personal and sensitive research data responsibly.

> [!NOTE]
> Adapted from a review of existing RDM courses compiled at Erasmus University Rotterdam (2026).
> Course details are paraphrased from the providers' pages; schedules, links and fees change over time.
> This page is not legal advice: consult your institution's data protection officer for specific cases.

## Finland

### Tampere University
- :office: [Processing and Safeguarding Research Data Involving Special Categories of Personal Data](https://libguides.tuni.fi/open-science-trainings/data-management) — a three-part online training.
    - **Core topics:** What special categories of personal data are and what is required to process them; handling these data at different stages of research; challenges in data collection, transcription and translation, and which software to use or avoid; secure storage options, including Tampere University's SDAE service; encryption policies and secure file transfer, such as encryption and Funet FileSender; risks and opportunities of artificial intelligence in processing special-category data; distribution, publication and appropriate disposal of the material. The course introduces SD Connect for secure data storage and sharing and SD Desktop for secure data analysis, with demonstrations and an optional hands-on session.
    - **Learning objectives:** Understand the requirements for processing special categories of personal data and the practical solutions for doing so securely throughout the research lifecycle.
    - **Resources:** Three one-hour online sessions; no prior technical expertise is required.
- :office: [Handling personal information in research](https://libguides.tuni.fi/open-science-trainings/data-management) — a three-part online training.
    - **Core topics:** What personal information means; anonymisation and pseudonymisation; the basic principles and key concepts of data protection and data minimisation; guidelines and regulations that affect the use of artificial intelligence; where to find additional instructions and help.
    - **Learning objectives:** Define what personal information means; understand what anonymisation and pseudonymisation involve; master the basic principles and key concepts of data protection and minimisation; comply with guidelines and regulations affecting the use of AI.
    - **Resources:** Three one-hour online sessions.
- :office: [Workshop on anonymizing research data](https://libguides.tuni.fi/open-science-trainings/data-management) — a one-hour online training.
    - **Core topics:** Anonymising research data in a pragmatic way, covering both quantitative and qualitative textual material; the difference between personal and anonymous data; anonymisation of quantitative data; anonymisation of qualitative data.
    - **Resources:** One-hour online training.

- :office: [Data anonymisation training](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/research/services-researchers/data-support/rdm-courses-workshops) — part of the University of Helsinki's RDM course programme.

## Related courses

Several general RDM and ethics courses also discuss privacy and anonymisation:
- [Stewarding software](../stewarding_data/software.md) — Radboud University's research skills programme covers GDPR, personal data and anonymisation.
- [RDM fundamentals](../basics/rdm_fundamentals.md) — University of Turku covers data protection; University of Eastern Finland covers personal data and legislation; University of Helsinki's RDM Basics covers ethical and legal compliance.
- [Ethics of Human Data](ethics_human_data.md) — Aalto University and Tampere University discuss informed consent and the ethical handling of human data.
- [FAIR principles](../stewarding_data/fair_principles.md) — the Maastricht University qualitative FAIR courses discuss how anonymisation affects data access levels.
