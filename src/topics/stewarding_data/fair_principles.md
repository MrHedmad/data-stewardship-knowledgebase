# FAIR principles

The FAIR principles describe how research data should be made **F**indable, **A**ccessible, **I**nteroperable and **R**eusable.
They are not a standard or a checklist, but a set of guidelines for making data more useful to both people and machines.

This page lists courses that focus on implementing FAIR principles in practice.

> [!NOTE]
> Adapted from a review of existing RDM courses compiled at Erasmus University Rotterdam (2026).
> Course details are paraphrased from the providers' pages; schedules, links and fees change over time.

## Netherlands

### Maastricht University
- :office: [Qualitative FAIR Data](https://library.maastrichtuniversity.nl/course/qualitative-fair-data/) — a two-hour online training.
    - **Core topics:** Applying the FAIR principles to qualitative research materials such as interview transcripts, focus groups and policy notes, so that they can be discovered and reused. The course covers relevant regulations and community standards for qualitative research, and how different levels of processing, such as raw versus anonymised data, affect the access levels that can be applied, such as open or restricted access.
    - **Learning objectives:** Understand the relevance of reusing qualitative data in the context of Open Science; be aware of the challenges in qualitative data reuse; understand which kinds of data and files should be made FAIR; be aware of current legislation and university regulations.
    - **Resources:** Online training materials.
- :office: [Qualitative FAIR Data (two-day workshop)](https://maastrichtu-library.github.io/qualitative-FAIR-data/) — two full in-person days.
    - **Core topics:** The theme of "Circular Research Data", inspired by the transition from a linear to a circular socio-economic model. A significant share of research output has followed a linear model: take the data, analyse and publish it, then dispose of it. The workshop discusses how to move to a circular model that acknowledges sustainability, and walks through the six steps of RDM following the FAIR principles in the context of Open Science, using real-world examples.
    - **Learning objectives:** Understand the importance of a "Research Digital Object"; explore open-source tools for interoperability that make research digital objects sustainable; learn what "Data Terms of Use" and "Data Access Protocols" are and how to create them; review the differences and similarities of "Data Descriptions" across scientific fields and discuss how to standardise these practices; understand what "Rich Metadata" means on the Semantic Web and how it relates to sustainable research output for future researchers.
    - **Exercises:** Exercises for each topic during the workshop.
    - **Resources:** Course platform, course materials and teachers.

## International

### The Carpentries
- :hammer: :beginner: [Six Steps to FAIR Implementation (with examples)](https://carpentries-incubator.github.io/FAIR-research-data-coursebook/) — an online training coursebook with no meetings.
    - **Core topics:** A low-entry-level coursebook that teaches the implementation of FAIR principles through examples that researchers can understand and apply immediately. Topics include the "Research Digital Object", open-source tools for interoperability, "Data Terms of Use" and "Data Access Protocols", "Data Descriptions" across scientific fields, and "Rich Metadata" on the Semantic Web.
    - **Learning objectives:** Work through six steps: set up your own terms (Data Terms of Use), speak the same language (Data Descriptions), share securely (Data Access Protocols), publish and preserve (Data Archiving), make machines work for you (Rich Metadata), and reuse responsibly (Data Reusing).
    - **Exercises:** Small exercises at the end of each topic.
    - **Resources:** Webpage.

## Related courses

FAIR principles are also covered in several general RDM and data archival courses:
- [RDM fundamentals](../basics/rdm_fundamentals.md) — TU Delft's RDM 101 includes FAIR assessment and implementation; Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam covers FAIR data and interoperability; University of Duisburg-Essen covers FAIR in practice; University of Turku and University of Eastern Finland include FAIR in their basic courses; Leiden University offers a module on FAIR data implementation.
- [Data Archival](data_archival.md) — Maastricht University's DataverseNL training covers metadata quality, access controls and reusable datasets.
- [Data Privacy](../policy_and_legal/data_privacy.md) — explains how anonymisation affects the access levels that can be applied to FAIR data.
